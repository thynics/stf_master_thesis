\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter evaluates the proposed scheduler on CUDASTF multi-GPU task graphs. The main goal of the evaluation is to determine whether Locality-Aware Residual HEFT reduces unnecessary logical-data movement relative to HEFT, and whether the observed runtime behavior is consistent with the data-movement mechanism predicted by the method. The chapter also reports standard benchmark sanity checks, a negative result for a naive slack scheduler, explainability-oriented metrics, and historical DVFS results.

The strongest empirical evidence in this thesis is the placement result on locality-sensitive benchmarks. In these experiments, Locality-Aware Residual HEFT improves runtime by reducing owner switches, copy events, copy bytes, and copy time. The DVFS results are reported more conservatively: they validate that the CUDASTF runtime can propagate scheduler-selected frequency decisions to execution, but final energy claims require measured frequency profiles and cleaned long-running energy experiments.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{subsec:results-metrics}

For placement, the primary runtime metric is mean wall time:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{wall}}(b,s),
\end{equation}
where \(b\) is a benchmark and \(s\) is a scheduler. Normalized runtime is computed against HEFT:
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{norm}}(b,s)
=
\frac{T_{\mathrm{wall}}(b,s)}
{T_{\mathrm{wall}}(b,\mathrm{HEFT})}.
\end{equation}
The relative improvement over HEFT is:
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_{\mathrm{rel}}(b,s)
=
100
\left(
\frac{T_{\mathrm{wall}}(b,s)}
{T_{\mathrm{wall}}(b,\mathrm{HEFT})}
-
1
\right)\%.
\end{equation}
A negative value of \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{rel}}\) indicates a runtime improvement.

The data-movement metrics are:
\begin{equation}
\left(
N_{\mathrm{switch}},
N_{\mathrm{copy}},
B_{\mathrm{copy}},
T_{\mathrm{copy}}
\right),
\end{equation}
where \(N_{\mathrm{switch}}\) is the number of logical-data owner switches, \(N_{\mathrm{copy}}\) is the number of copy events, \(B_{\mathrm{copy}}\) is total copy volume, and \(T_{\mathrm{copy}}\) is total predicted or measured copy time.

For DVFS, the energy metrics are:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{EDP}
=
E T,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ED}^{2}\mathrm{P}
=
E T^2,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{E}^{2}\mathrm{DP}
=
E^2 T,
\end{equation}
where \(E\) is application-level energy and \(T\) is runtime.

\subsection{Result Mechanism}
\label{subsec:results-mechanism}

The runtime improvement aligns with the intended mechanism of the method. HEFT optimizes the local earliest finish time:
\begin{equation}
g_0(t)
=
\mathop{\mathrm{arg\,min}}_{g}
\mathrm{EFT}(t,g).
\end{equation}
However, in a logical-data runtime, this local decision can move a task away from the current logical-data owner. This may produce repeated data movement even when the local EFT advantage is small.

Locality-Aware Residual HEFT accepts a non-HEFT GPU only when:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{copy}}(t,g)
\geq
\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}(p,q(t)),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{copy}}(t,g)
\geq
\tau_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,q(t)),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{eft}}(t,g)
\leq
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{abs}}(p,q(t)),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\mathrm{eft}}(t,g)
\leq
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}(p,q(t)).
\end{equation}
The data-movement results show that these constraints reduce owner switches and copy traffic. The main result is therefore not only a runtime speedup, but also a mechanism-level explanation for that speedup.

\centering
\fbox{
\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\centering
\textbf{Observed placement mechanism}

\vspace{0.3cm}

\begin{tabular}{c}
HEFT local min-EFT placement \\
\(\Downarrow\) \\

\(\Downarrow\) \\

\(\Downarrow\) \\

\label{fig:results-mechanism}

For Domain Decomposition Halo Exchange, HEFT repeatedly chooses locally earlier GPUs, which causes halo data to move between devices. The residual schedulers suppress these moves by preserving ownership when the copy-saving certificate passes. Bandit is more aggressive and therefore reduces owner switches more strongly, while LAR-HEFT is more conservative but still improves runtime.

For Blocked Low-Rank Tile Update, repeated updates create hot logical data. HEFT may move update tasks away from the current modified owner. LAR-HEFT and Bandit preserve locality for these updates when the local EFT loss remains bounded, which explains why both produce the same owner-switch and copy-time reductions on this benchmark.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:results-threats}

\subsection{Internal Validity}
\label{subsec:results-internal-validity}

Task cost estimates and copy-bandwidth estimates may be inaccurate. The MSI-style residency model is intentionally lightweight and may not fully capture topology-dependent bandwidth, copy-engine contention, or overlap between communication and computation. Logging may perturb runtime if full detailed logs are enabled. Shadow evaluation approximates paired policy behavior, but it may not perfectly match future runtime ordering under all conditions.

\subsection{External Validity}
\label{subsec:results-external-validity}

The strongest improvements are observed on locality-sensitive bad cases. Other workloads may show smaller gains or near-neutral behavior, as seen in the standard benchmark sanity check. Results may vary with GPU topology, interconnect bandwidth, GPU count, benchmark scale, and CUDASTF implementation details. The current scope is single-node multi-GPU execution and does not cover multi-node clusters.

\subsection{Construct Validity}
\label{subsec:results-construct-validity}

Runtime improvement is supported by copy metrics, but copy time alone may not capture all communication effects. Owner-switch reduction is a useful proxy for logical-data locality, but it is not the only determinant of runtime. DVFS results are currently historical and require measured frequency profiles before strong final energy claims can be made.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{subsec:results-reproducibility}

The final reproducibility package should include the CUDASTF commit hash, benchmark commands, environment variables, hardware details, number of repetitions, warm-up policy, log parsing scripts, and all raw or summarized run tables. The key environment variables include:

\section{Results Summary}
\label{sec:results-summary}

The main placement results show that Locality-Aware Residual HEFT improves runtime on locality-sensitive CUDASTF benchmarks by reducing unnecessary logical-data movement. On Domain Decomposition Halo Exchange, LAR-HEFT improves runtime by \(7.498\%\) relative to HEFT and reduces owner switches by \(47.795\%\). On Blocked Low-Rank Tile Update, LAR-HEFT improves runtime by \(10.368\%\) and reduces owner switches by \(67.337\%\). These results support the thesis claim that HEFT's local min-EFT objective can fragment logical-data flow, and that a certificate-based HEFT-residual locality gate can reduce this pathology.

The standard benchmark results are more mixed and should be interpreted as compatibility evidence rather than as the primary performance claim. Cholesky improves, CG is nearly neutral, and FDTD and MiniWeather show small changes. The negative slack-scheduler result demonstrates that naive slack-aware placement is insufficient and supports the need for a HEFT-relative copy-saving certificate with bounded EFT loss.

The DVFS experiments validate a CUDASTF-integrated energy-control path and show promising historical energy/EDP trade-offs on MixedPCG and Cholesky. However, final energy claims require measured per-GPU frequency profiles, cleaned long-running benchmarks, and explicit transition-hiding metrics. The thesis therefore treats locality-aware placement as the main validated contribution and DVFS as an implemented energy-aware extension.
